## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Acoustically Sound PEQ Optimization Engine (Priority: P1)

As an audio enthusiast and calibrator, I want the PEQ calculation engine to accurately detect genuine room resonance peaks (and never place cuts or notches into acoustic dips, cancellations, or boundary nulls), so that the resulting filter parameters for the left front speaker (Front L) and right front speaker (Front R) follow established acoustic principles (Dr. Floyd Toole / AES standard) and smoothly converge toward the target curve.

**Why this priority**: Erroneous filter calculations—such as placing heavy cuts of -6 dB to -10.5 dB in frequencies where the room already has dips—completely hollow out the lower frequencies (60 Hz – 300 Hz), ruin bass impact, and cause severe divergence between the measured response and the target curve. Correcting the core optimization logic is the foundational requirement.

**Independent Test**: Execute the optimization algorithm on the empirical room measurements. Verify that Front L filters only apply negative cuts at genuine resonant peaks where measured SPL exceeds target SPL (`measured > target`), never cut into dips, calculate accurate Q factors based on physical bandwidth (in Hz), and achieve a monotonic reduction in RMS target deviation without excessive cumulative attenuation.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** room measurement data with a resonant peak (e.g., +4 dB to +6 dB above target around 125 Hz on Front L) and a dip (e.g., -5 dB below target at 62 Hz or 80 Hz), **When** the PEQ optimizer calculates the 7 parametric bands, **Then** it allocates an attenuation filter to the peak at 125 Hz and allocates no cut (0.0 dB gain) to the dip at 62 Hz or 80 Hz.
2. **Given** any candidate filter generated by the optimizer, **When** calculating filter bandwidth and Q factor, **Then** the bandwidth is computed in Hertz from the frequency axis (not raw array indices), resulting in realistic Q factors between 0.5 and 5.0 (avoiding artificial Q = 10.08 saturation on broadband ripples).
3. **Given** the 7 discrete Yamaha PEQ bands for Front L, **When** the optimizer finishes, **Then** the cumulative sum of negative cuts across the modal band (30 Hz – 300 Hz) does not hollow out the low end, and the predicted post-PEQ response is within ±2.5 dB of the target curve in the modal region.

As a calibrator, I want the automated calibration workflow to ingest the canonical Sweet Spot measurement (Punto 1) synchronized with the multi-point spatial average, applying consistent 1 kHz acoustic level normalization, so that the optimization is computed on accurate, unskewed room data.

**Why this priority**: Discrepancies between different data captures (e.g., loading an outdated measurement file with an anomalous dip instead of the validated multi-point session) or omitting consistent 1 kHz normalization causes artificial level offsets that deceive the optimizer into thinking the entire bass region is elevated or depressed.

**Independent Test**: Run the automated calibration pipeline (`scripts/auto_calibrate.py`) and verify that it sources `medicion_punto_1.npz` (Sweet Spot) and `medicion_promedio_espacial.npz` (Spatial Average), normalizes both to the 1 kHz acoustic reference level, and produces mathematically reproducible filter configurations matching the target profile.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a multi-point calibration session, **When** the calibration optimizer loads baseline data, **Then** it loads `data/medicion_punto_1.npz` as the authoritative Sweet Spot reference and `data/medicion_promedio_espacial.npz` as the spatial average.
2. **Given** measured frequency responses with different recording gain offsets, **When** preparing data for error evaluation, **Then** both measured responses and spatial averages are normalized to 0.0 dB at 1 kHz relative to the target curve.
3. **Given** target profile configurations in `config/targets.json`, **When** optimizing filters, **Then** high-frequency filters (> 500 Hz) preserve the documented loudspeaker acoustic voicing (such as the 2.5 kHz crossover compensation) and never apply arbitrary cuts or boosts above the Schroeder transition frequency.

**Why this priority**: The user needs clear, objective confirmation that the sound has improved and that the verification sweep matches the expected target. **Acceptance Scenarios**:

2. **Given** a post-calibration response that shows severe bass hollowing (e.g., > 6 dB dip below target in the 60 Hz – 150 Hz band), **When** evaluating certification criteria, **Then** the verification status flags the response as requiring review rather than granting unconditional S-TIER certification.
3. **Given** the web calibration dashboard and generated PDF reports, **When** viewing the post-calibration verification tab, **Then** the visual curves and data tables show close tracking of the target curve for both Front L and Front R channels.

### Edge Cases

- **Non-Minimum Phase Nulls (Boundary Reflections / SBIR)**: The room has a boundary cancellation dip at 80 Hz or 250 Hz. The optimizer must recognize that boosting or cutting a cancellation null cannot restore phase coherence, and MUST clamp filter gain to 0.0 dB at that frequency.
- **Asymmetric Room Boundaries (Corner Loading vs. Open Space)**: Front L is positioned in an open living area while Front R is closer to a side boundary (or vice-versa). The optimizer must optimize each channel independently, allowing Front L and Front R to select different discrete center frequencies and Q factors matching their specific modal fingerprints.
- **Adjacent Filter Compounding**: Two discrete Yamaha frequencies (e.g. 125.0 Hz and 157.5 Hz) are selected by the optimizer. The optimizer must evaluate the combined biquad transfer function to ensure the overlapping skirts do not produce an excessive cumulative dip exceeding -12.0 dB.
- **Schroeder Transition Frequency Clamping**: Above 500 Hz, room acoustic modal behavior transitions to ray acoustics. The optimizer must enforce a strict zero-boost rule (`gain <= 0.0 dB`) and strictly limit cuts above 500 Hz to predefined acoustic corrections (e.g., speaker crossover dips).

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The PEQ optimizer MUST detect modal resonances exclusively at frequencies where the measured SPL exceeds the target curve by at least +1.5 dB (`measured_SPL - target_SPL >= +1.5 dB`), preventing any filter cuts on dips or nulls.
- **FR-002**: The PEQ optimizer MUST compute the -3 dB bandwidth of detected peaks in physical frequency units (Hertz) based on the frequency vector, snapping the resulting Q factor to the closest valid Yamaha discrete value between 0.500 and 10.080.
- **FR-003**: The PEQ optimizer MUST limit the maximum attenuation of any single filter to -12.0 dB and the maximum boost below 500 Hz to +3.0 dB, with 0.0 dB boost permitted above 500 Hz.
- **FR-004**: The PEQ optimizer MUST calculate the combined multi-filter biquad response at each optimization step, ensuring that adjacent filters do not compound into an excessive cumulative cut deeper than -12.0 dB at any frequency point.
- **FR-005**: The calibration workflow MUST source the baseline reference from `medicion_punto_1.npz` (Sweet Spot) and the spatial average from `medicion_promedio_espacial.npz`, ensuring both are aligned to a 0.0 dB reference at 1.0 kHz.
- **FR-006**: The PEQ optimizer MUST support channel-independent center frequencies and Q factors for Front L and Front R to properly address room acoustic boundary asymmetry.
- **FR-007**: High-frequency filter bands (> 500 Hz) defined in `config/targets.json` (such as the 2.52 kHz vocal clarity crossover compensation) MUST be preserved in the final filter set unless overridden by an explicit acoustic constraint.
- **FR-008**: The verification module (`scripts/verify_calibration.py`) MUST evaluate target convergence using residual RMS error in the modal band (30 Hz – 500 Hz) and reject/flag any calibration that introduces an artificial dip deeper than 4.0 dB below the target curve in the 60 Hz – 200 Hz region.
- **FR-009**: The web calibration server and automated calibration scripts MUST produce synchronized, identical filter tables across the CLI, web API, and generated PDF reports.

### Key Entities

- **Modal Resonance Peak**: A room acoustic standing wave characterized by a center frequency ($f_0$ in Hz), an elevation in sound pressure level above the target curve ($\Delta \text{SPL} > 0$ in dB), and an acoustic Q factor ($\frac{f_0}{\Delta f_{-3\text{dB}}}$).
- **Yamaha PEQ Filter Band**: A discrete parametric biquad peaking filter constrained to the Yamaha RX-V673 DSP specifications: 28 discrete frequencies (31.3 Hz to 16.0 kHz), 14 discrete Q factors (0.500 to 10.080), and gains from -12.0 dB to +3.0 dB in 0.5 dB steps.
- **Target Curve Profile**: An acoustic target curve (e.g. Harman Room Target, B&K 1974, Dirac Live Modern Stereo) specifying the desired frequency response slope, low-frequency acoustic roll-off matching the Q Acoustics 3020i (64 Hz, -3 dB), and high-frequency shelf.
- **Acoustic Verification Metric Set**: Numerical metrics including residual RMS error from target, peak modal attenuation, inter-channel stereo balance ($|L - R|$ mean deviation), and bass suckout penalty.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: Front L and Front R calculated PEQ filters achieve a residual RMS deviation from the target curve of less than 2.5 dB in the modal region (30 Hz – 500 Hz) upon simulated and measured post-calibration evaluation.
- **SC-002**: Zero negative filter cuts (0.0 dB) are assigned to frequencies where the measured room response is below the target curve (100% elimination of false peak cuts in bass dips/nulls).
- **SC-003**: The peak room resonance in Front L (around 119 Hz – 125 Hz) is attenuated by at least 6.0 dB, resolving the corner-boundary boom without reducing adjacent sub-bass (60 Hz – 90 Hz) output by more than 1.5 dB.
- **SC-004**: Execution time for the 7-band stereo optimization remains under 500 milliseconds on the host system.
- **SC-005**: All calculated filter parameters match the exact discrete constraints of the Yamaha RX-V673 DSP, successfully deploying to hardware and passing readback verification via HTTP XML API without syntax or out-of-bounds errors.

## Assumptions

- The physical speakers (Q Acoustics 3020i) have a natural low-frequency acoustic roll-off (-3 dB at 64 Hz, 4th order high-pass reflex behavior) which is accounted for in the target curve.
- Room modes below the Schroeder frequency (~300 Hz – 500 Hz) are minimum-phase and can be effectively corrected using parametric notch filters; deep boundary cancellations are non-minimum phase and must not be equalized with positive boost or aggressive cuts.
- The Sweet Spot measurement (`medicion_punto_1.npz`) accurately captures the primary listener ear-height position, while the 5-point spatial average (`medicion_promedio_espacial.npz`) provides the weighted spatial context (80% Sweet Spot / 20% spatial average).
